🐾 Origami Animals

Fold a creature from one square

🐶 Origami Dog  Easy · about 5 minutes
Two folds and a face. The first origami almost everybody learns, and still one of the best.
You need: 1 square of paper (15 cm is perfect) · A felt pen or crayon
  1. Put the square on the table like a diamond, one corner pointing at you.
  2. Fold the top corner down to the bottom corner. You now have a triangle.
  3. Turn the triangle so the long edge is at the top.
  4. Fold the left corner down and out to make one floppy ear. Do the same on the right.
  5. Fold the very bottom point up a little way — that is the dog's chin.
  6. Draw two eyes and a nose, and give your dog a name.
Tip: Press every fold flat with your thumbnail before you make the next one.
🐱 Origami Cat  Easy · about 5 minutes
The dog's neighbour: same start, pointy ears instead of floppy ones.
You need: 1 square of paper · A felt pen
  1. Lay the square as a diamond and fold the top corner down to the bottom. You have a triangle.
  2. Turn it so the long edge is at the top.
  3. Fold the left and right corners UP this time, so two pointy ears stand above the head.
  4. Fold the bottom point up behind the head to make the chin flat.
  5. Draw two eyes, a small triangle nose and three whiskers on each side.
Tip: Ears up means cat, ears down means dog — that single change is the whole trick.
🐸 Jumping Frog  Medium · about 12 minutes
Press its back and it really leaps. Have a jumping race across the kitchen table.
You need: 1 rectangle of paper or thin card (a filing card is ideal) · Felt pens
  1. Fold the top left corner across to the right edge, crease it, and open it out. Do the same with the top right corner. You have made an X.
  2. Turn the paper over and fold straight across the middle of the X, then open it out again.
  3. Turn it back. Push the two sides of the X inwards so the top collapses into a flat triangle.
  4. Fold the two bottom corners of that triangle up to the top point — those are the front legs.
  5. Fold the long sides of the body in to the middle so the frog is narrow.
  6. Fold the bottom edge up to the top, then fold half of it back down. That zigzag is the spring.
  7. Turn it over, draw two eyes, then press down on its back and let go.
Tip: If it flips over instead of jumping forward, your zigzag spring is too tight — make it a little looser.
🕊️ Paper Crane  Hard · about 25 minutes
The most famous fold in the world. It takes a few goes to learn — and then you have it forever.
You need: 1 square of thin paper (15 cm) · A flat table · Patience
  1. Fold the square in half both ways, open it, turn it over, then fold corner to corner both ways and open it. You should see a star of creases.
  2. Push the four sides together so the paper collapses into a small square with a loose corner at the bottom. This is the 'square base'.
  3. With the loose corner at the bottom, fold the two lower edges of the top layer in to the centre line, like a kite.
  4. Fold the top triangle down over them, crease it well, then open all three folds back out.
  5. Lift the bottom loose corner all the way up and press the sides in, so it flattens into a long diamond. Turn over and do the same on the back.
  6. Fold the lower edges in to the centre again on both sides — the shape gets very narrow.
  7. Pull the two thin points out sideways and crease them so they stay: they are the head and tail.
  8. Fold a tiny bit at the tip of one point down to make the beak, then gently pull the wings apart.
Tip: Do not rush step 5. If the diamond will not lie flat, your creases from step 4 are not sharp enough.
🦋 Origami Butterfly  Medium · about 10 minutes
Fold a dozen in different colours and tape them up the wall in a flying line.
You need: 1 square of paper · String and tape if you want to hang them
  1. Fold the square in half one way and open it, then the other way and open it.
  2. Turn it over and fold it corner to corner both ways, opening it each time.
  3. Push the two side creases inwards so the square collapses into a smaller triangle.
  4. With the open points at the bottom, fold the two bottom corners of the top layer up to the top point.
  5. Turn it over. Fold the bottom point up so it sticks out over the top edge, then fold its tip back down.
  6. Fold the whole thing in half backwards down the middle, then let it spring open a little so the wings sit apart.
  7. Curl two thin strips of paper and tape them on for antennae.
Tip: Different sized squares make a whole family — try 20 cm, 15 cm and 10 cm.
🦊 Origami Fox  Easy · about 6 minutes
A sharp-eared fox from one triangle. Make two and they can talk to each other.
You need: 1 square of orange paper · A black felt pen
  1. Fold the square corner to corner to make a triangle, with the long edge at the bottom.
  2. Fold the triangle in half from left to right, then open it again so you can see the middle crease.
  3. Fold the left and right points UP to meet the top point — those are the ears.
  4. Turn the whole thing over.
  5. Draw a black nose on the bottom point and two eyes above it.
Tip: Make the ears slightly uneven on purpose and your fox will look cheeky.
🦢 Paper Swan  Hard · about 18 minutes
A long curved neck made only from folds. It stands up on the table all by itself.
You need: 1 square of paper (white or pale blue)
  1. Fold the square corner to corner, crease it hard, and open it out. That middle line guides everything.
  2. Fold the two top edges in to the middle line so you have a kite shape.
  3. Fold those same two long edges in to the middle line AGAIN. The kite gets very thin.
  4. Fold the whole thin kite in half backwards along the middle line.
  5. Hold the fat end. Bend the thin end upwards and crease it — that is the neck.
  6. Bend the very tip of the neck forwards and crease it again — that is the head.
  7. Fold a tiny triangle at the very tip for the beak, then stand the swan on its flat bottom.
Tip: Thin paper is much easier here — by step 3 you are folding eight layers at once.
🐠 Origami Fish  Easy · about 7 minutes
Fold a whole shoal in different colours and stick them on a window like an aquarium.
You need: 1 square of paper · Felt pens · Sticky tack if you want to put them on a window
  1. Fold the square corner to corner both ways, opening it each time, so you have an X of creases.
  2. Fold the square in half sideways and open it out.
  3. Push the two sides in so the paper collapses into a triangle with two loose flaps inside.
  4. Fold the left and right points of the top layer up to the top point.
  5. Turn it round so the long straight edge is the fish's back.
  6. Pull one of the back points out a little and crease it to make the tail fin.
  7. Draw a big round eye and some scales.
Tip: Cut a wavy edge on the tail with scissors before you start and the finished fish looks much fishier.
🐧 Origami Penguin  Medium · about 10 minutes
A little round penguin that stands on its own two feet.
You need: 1 square of paper, black one side and white the other if you have it
  1. Fold the square corner to corner to make a triangle, then open it back out.
  2. Fold the left and right corners in to the middle crease so you have a kite.
  3. Turn it over and fold the whole kite in half along the middle crease.
  4. Stand it on the table with the fat end down. Bend the thin top point forward and crease it — that is the head.
  5. Fold a small triangle at the very tip forwards for the beak.
  6. Fold a small triangle at each side of the bottom outwards for the feet, so it stands up.
  7. Draw two eyes just behind the beak.
Tip: Colour the beak and feet orange with a crayon; it takes ten seconds and makes it look finished.
🐰 Origami Rabbit  Medium · about 12 minutes
Tall ears, round cheeks, and a puff of tissue for a tail.
You need: 1 square of paper · A small piece of tissue or cotton wool for the tail · Glue
  1. Fold the square in half to make a triangle, long edge at the bottom.
  2. Fold the two bottom corners up to the top point so you have a small square standing on its corner.
  3. Fold those two new points back out and downwards to make the ears stand away from the head.
  4. Turn the rabbit over.
  5. Fold the very bottom point up behind, so the rabbit sits flat and steady.
  6. Draw two eyes, a little nose and three whiskers each side.
  7. Scrunch the tissue into a ball and glue it on the back for a tail.
Tip: Pinch a fold down the middle of each ear and they will stand up instead of flopping.

🌸 Flowers &amp; Nature

Blooms, leaves and growing things

🌷 Paper Tulip  Easy · about 8 minutes
A flower that never needs watering. Make a whole bunch for someone who deserves one.
You need: 1 square of coloured paper for the flower · 1 square of green paper for the stem · Glue
  1. Fold the coloured square corner to corner into a triangle, long edge at the bottom.
  2. Fold the left corner up so it points past the top, then do the same with the right corner.
  3. Turn it over — you have a tulip head.
  4. For the stem, fold the green square corner to corner, then roll it tightly from one end into a thin stick.
  5. Fold the last corner over and glue it so the stem does not unroll.
  6. Cut two leaf shapes from the leftover green paper and glue them to the stem.
  7. Glue the stem to the back of the flower head.
Tip: Five tulips tied with a ribbon makes a proper bouquet.
🌺 Tissue Paper Flower  Easy · about 10 minutes
Big, fluffy and forgiving — the messier your folds, the fuller the flower looks.
You need: 6 sheets of tissue paper (or thin napkins) · 1 pipe cleaner or a twist tie · Scissors
  1. Stack all six sheets neatly on top of each other.
  2. Fold the whole stack like a fan: over, under, over, under, in strips about 3 cm wide.
  3. Squeeze the middle of the folded strip and tie it tightly with the pipe cleaner.
  4. Round off both ends of the strip with scissors, or cut them into points for a spikier flower.
  5. Open the fan out into a fat circle.
  6. Very gently pull the layers up one at a time towards the middle, starting from the top.
Tip: Pull the layers slowly. Tissue tears if you hurry, and one torn layer spoils the puff.
🌹 Rolled Paper Rose  Hard · about 15 minutes
Cut one spiral, roll it up, and a rose appears. It looks like magic and it nearly is.
You need: 1 square of red or pink paper · Scissors · Glue · A green straw or rolled paper for the stem
  1. Draw a circle on the paper — around a mug is perfect.
  2. Cut out the circle.
  3. Starting at the edge, cut a spiral round and round towards the middle, keeping the strip about 2 cm wide. Stop when you reach the centre and leave a small round tab there.
  4. Make the spiral wavy rather than straight and the petals will look softer.
  5. Start at the outside end and roll the strip up tightly between your finger and thumb.
  6. When you reach the middle, let it relax a little so it opens out.
  7. Put glue on the round tab in the centre and press the rolled flower down onto it. Hold for a minute.
  8. Glue the finished rose to the top of the stem.
Tip: Roll tightly for a bud and loosely for a full open rose. Make one of each.
🍃 Leaf Garland  Easy · about 12 minutes
A trail of leaves to hang along a shelf or across a window.
You need: Green paper in two or three shades · Scissors · String (about 2 m) · Glue stick
  1. Fold a sheet of green paper in half, then in half again, and again.
  2. Draw one leaf shape on the top layer, right up against the folded edge.
  3. Cut it out through all the layers — you get eight leaves from one drawing.
  4. Repeat with the other shades until you have about thirty leaves.
  5. Draw a line down the middle of each leaf, then fold gently along it so the leaf is not flat.
  6. Lay the string out straight and put a dab of glue near the top of each leaf.
  7. Press the leaves onto the string one after another, alternating the colours.
Tip: Leave a bare 20 cm of string at each end for tying it up.
🌼 Paper Daisy Chain  Medium · about 15 minutes
The daisy chain you make in summer, except this one lasts all year.
You need: White paper · Yellow paper · Scissors · Glue stick
  1. Cut white paper into strips about 2 cm wide and 12 cm long. You need six per flower.
  2. Take one strip, bring both ends into the middle to make a loop, and glue the ends down. That is one petal.
  3. Make six petals for each flower.
  4. Arrange six petals in a circle with all the glued ends touching in the middle, and glue them together.
  5. Cut a small yellow circle and glue it over the middle to hide the joins.
  6. Make as many flowers as you like, then glue them to each other side by side to make the chain.
Tip: Press each finished flower under a heavy book for five minutes so it dries flat.
🌳 Stand-Up Paper Tree  Medium · about 14 minutes
Two flat trees slot together and suddenly it is a 3D tree that stands on the table.
You need: Thick green paper or card · Scissors · A pencil · Brown paper for the trunk
  1. Fold a sheet of green card in half and draw half a tree shape against the fold.
  2. Cut it out and open it — you have a whole tree.
  3. Make a second tree exactly the same size. Trace the first one so they match.
  4. On the first tree, cut a straight slot from the TOP down to the middle.
  5. On the second tree, cut a straight slot from the BOTTOM up to the middle.
  6. Slide the two slots together so the trees cross in an X.
  7. Cut two small brown rectangles and glue them around the bottom as the trunk.
  8. Stand it up and check it does not wobble. Trim the bottom flat if it does.
Tip: The slots must be exactly as wide as the card is thick, or the tree will be loose.
🌻 Paper Sunflower  Medium · about 14 minutes
A big cheerful face with a middle made from scrunched-up paper balls.
You need: Yellow paper · Brown paper · A paper plate or a circle of card · Glue · Scissors
  1. Cut about twenty petal shapes from the yellow paper, each around 10 cm long.
  2. Pinch the bottom of each petal and glue the pinch shut so the petal curves.
  3. Glue the petals around the edge of the circle of card, pointing outwards.
  4. Glue a second ring of petals just inside the first, so no gaps show.
  5. Tear the brown paper into small pieces and scrunch each one into a tight little ball.
  6. Cover the middle of the circle with glue and press the brown balls on, packed close together.
  7. Leave it flat to dry for twenty minutes.
Tip: Two shades of yellow — one for each ring of petals — makes it look far more real.
🦋 Butterfly Mobile  Medium · about 20 minutes
Butterflies that turn slowly above a bed. Hang it where a draught can reach it.
You need: Coloured paper · Scissors · Thread or thin string · Sticky tape · A garden stick or a straw — or a wire coat hanger, if a grown-up bends it and checks the ends are not sharp
  1. Fold a piece of paper in half and draw half a butterfly against the fold.
  2. Cut it out and open it. Make about eight, in different colours and sizes.
  3. Fold each butterfly gently down the middle again so the wings sit up in a V.
  4. Cut eight pieces of thread, all different lengths between 15 cm and 40 cm.
  5. Tape one end of each thread to the back of a butterfly, right on the middle fold.
  6. Tie the other ends along the stick, spaced out, with the longest in the middle.
  7. Tie one more piece of thread to each end of the stick and join them above to hang it.
  8. Hold it up and slide the threads along the stick until it balances.
Tip: If one side dips, slide a butterfly along the stick rather than adding anything.
🌵 Paper Cactus in a Pot  Medium · about 15 minutes
A houseplant that survives absolutely anything, including being forgotten.
You need: Green paper · A small paper cup or a rolled paper pot · Scissors · Glue · Scrunched paper or a stone to weigh it down
  1. Cut four identical cactus shapes from green paper — a fat body with two arms.
  2. Fold each one exactly in half down the middle.
  3. Glue the right half of the first to the left half of the second.
  4. Keep going until all four are joined in a ring, then glue the last edge to the first.
  5. Open it out: the cactus is now round and stands up.
  6. Put the stone or scrunched paper in the bottom of the cup so it will not tip over.
  7. Glue the bottom of the cactus into the cup.
  8. Draw little white spines with a crayon, and add a paper flower on top if you like.
Tip: All four shapes must be the same size or the cactus will lean. Cut them together in a stack.
🌿 Paper Fern Frond  Easy · about 8 minutes
One long strip, snipped along both sides, curls into a feathery fern.
You need: Green paper · Scissors · A pencil
  1. Cut a strip of green paper about 5 cm wide and as long as the sheet.
  2. Fold the strip in half lengthways and crease it.
  3. Keeping it folded, snip in from the open edges towards the fold — about every 5 mm, stopping 5 mm short of the fold.
  4. Do this all the way along the strip.
  5. Open it out. You have a long stem with lots of thin leaves.
  6. Run each leaf over the edge of a pencil to make it curl.
  7. Bend the whole frond into a gentle curve.
Tip: Cut the snips at a slight angle rather than straight across and the fern looks much more alive.

💌 Cards &amp; Keepsakes

Things to give away

💗 Pop-Up Heart Card  Medium · about 12 minutes
Open the card and a heart jumps out at whoever you gave it to.
You need: 1 sheet of card for the outside · 1 sheet of red or pink paper · Scissors · Glue stick · Pens
  1. Fold the card in half and put it to one side — that is the outside.
  2. Fold the pink paper in half and draw half a heart against the fold.
  3. Cut it out and keep it folded.
  4. Cut two short parallel slits into the FOLD of the outer card, about 3 cm apart and 2 cm deep.
  5. Open the card and push that little tab through to the inside, then close the card and press it flat.
  6. Open it again — the tab now sticks out like a step.
  7. Put glue on the front of the tab and press one half of the heart onto it.
  8. Glue the other half of the heart to the other side of the tab. Close the card gently to check it folds flat.
Tip: If the heart shows when the card is shut, it is too big. Trim it and try again.
🖼️ Accordion Photo Card  Medium · about 15 minutes
A card that opens out into a long zigzag of pictures. Perfect for grandparents.
You need: A long strip of card (or two sheets taped end to end) · Photos or drawings · Glue stick · Ribbon
  1. Lay the strip flat and fold it in half.
  2. Fold each half back on itself, so the strip zigzags into four equal panels.
  3. Press all the folds hard, then open it out to check the zigzag stands up like a fan.
  4. Glue one photo or drawing onto each panel.
  5. Write a few words under each one — when it was, or who is in it.
  6. Fold it back up, wrap the ribbon around the middle, and tie a bow.
Tip: Fold first, glue second. Glueing first makes the folds crack straight through the picture.
💌 Thank-You Card &amp; Envelope  Easy · about 10 minutes
A card and the envelope it goes in, both from one sheet each. Everyone should be able to make these.
You need: 2 sheets of paper (one plain, one patterned) · Scissors · Glue stick · Pens
  1. Fold the plain sheet in half — that is the card. Decorate the front and write inside.
  2. For the envelope, put the second sheet down as a diamond, patterned side facing down.
  3. Put the folded card in the middle to check the size.
  4. Fold the left and right corners in over the card so they nearly meet.
  5. Fold the bottom corner up over both of them and glue along its edges.
  6. Slide the card in and fold the top corner down to close it.
  7. Write the name on the front.
Tip: Make the envelope around the card, never the other way round, and it always fits.
🤚 Handprint Card  Easy · about 8 minutes
Draw round your own hand. In ten years it will be the best thing in the drawer.
You need: Card · A pencil · Crayons or paint · Scissors
  1. Fold the card in half.
  2. Put your hand flat on the front with your thumb touching the folded edge.
  3. Draw carefully all the way round your hand with the pencil.
  4. Cut out the hand shape, but do NOT cut along the folded edge — that keeps the card joined.
  5. Open it up: two hands, joined at the thumb.
  6. Colour it in and write your message inside.
  7. Write your age and the date somewhere small on the back.
Tip: Keep the pencil upright while you draw round, or the hand comes out fat.
🎂 Pop-Up Cake Card  Hard · about 20 minutes
A three-layer cake that folds completely flat and springs up when the card opens.
You need: 1 sheet of card · Coloured paper · Ruler · Scissors · Glue stick
  1. Fold the card in half and open it again so you can see the middle crease.
  2. Cut a pair of parallel slits into the crease, 6 cm apart and 4 cm deep. That is the biggest cake layer.
  3. Cut a second pair inside the first, 4 cm apart and 3 cm deep.
  4. Cut a third pair inside those, 2 cm apart and 2 cm deep.
  5. Fold each tab forwards, then close the card and press flat so all three tabs pop inwards.
  6. Open the card — three steps stand up, biggest at the back.
  7. Cut coloured paper to cover the front of each step and glue it on.
  8. Cut thin strips for candles, glue them behind the top step, and add little flame shapes.
Tip: Measure the slits. This is one of the few paper projects where guessing really does not work.
👹 Corner Bookmark Monster  Medium · about 12 minutes
A little monster that sits on the corner of the page and eats it while you are not reading.
You need: 1 square of coloured paper · Scraps of white and black paper · Scissors · Glue stick
  1. Fold the square corner to corner into a triangle, long edge at the bottom.
  2. Fold the left and right corners up to the top point, so you have a smaller square standing on its corner.
  3. Open those two folds back out.
  4. Take just the TOP layer of the top point and fold it down to the bottom edge.
  5. Now fold the left and right corners up again — and tuck them INTO the pocket you just made.
  6. Turn it over. You have a triangle with a pocket that slides onto a page corner.
  7. Cut zigzag teeth from white paper and glue them just inside the opening.
  8. Cut two white circles with black dots for eyes and glue them on top.
Tip: Tuck the corners deep into the pocket or the monster will not hold onto the page.
🖼️ Paper Photo Frame  Easy · about 10 minutes
A frame with a fold-out leg, so it stands up on a shelf on its own.
You need: Thick card · Scissors · Ruler · Glue · A photo or drawing
  1. Cut two rectangles of card the same size, a bit bigger than your picture.
  2. On the first, draw a window 1 cm smaller than the picture all the way round, and cut it out.
  3. Glue the two rectangles together along the bottom and both sides — but leave the TOP open.
  4. Slide the picture in through the top.
  5. Cut a long triangle of card for the stand.
  6. Fold 2 cm over at the top of the triangle and glue that flap to the back of the frame.
  7. Stand it up and bend the leg until it stops tipping backwards.
Tip: Decorate the frame BEFORE you put the photo in. It is much easier flat.
📓 Mini Memory Book  Medium · about 12 minutes
An eight-page book from ONE sheet of paper, with one clever cut. No staples, no glue.
You need: 1 sheet of paper · Scissors · Pens
  1. Fold the sheet in half the short way, and open it.
  2. Fold it in half the long way, and open it.
  3. Fold each long edge in to the middle crease, then open everything out. You should see eight rectangles.
  4. Fold the sheet in half the short way again.
  5. Cut along the middle crease from the FOLDED edge, but stop at the centre of the sheet. You have made a slit in the middle.
  6. Open the sheet out and fold it in half the long way.
  7. Hold both ends and push them towards each other — the slit opens into a diamond and the pages fold round into a book.
  8. Flatten it into a book shape and press the spine. Now fill in the pages.
Tip: Number the pages lightly in pencil before you cut, so you know which way up each one goes.
🔐 Secret Message Card  Medium · about 12 minutes
Folds shut into a little square with a hidden catch. Only someone who knows can open it.
You need: 1 square of paper · Pens · A sticker or a small paper heart
  1. Write your message in the very middle of the square.
  2. Fold all four corners in to the centre so they meet. The message is hidden.
  3. Turn it over.
  4. Fold all four corners in to the centre on this side too.
  5. Turn it back over and fold the whole thing in half.
  6. Unfold once, and you will find you can tuck the last corner under the flap opposite it. That is the catch.
  7. Decorate the outside and stick your sticker over the catch so it stays shut.
Tip: Write the message first. Once it is folded you cannot get back in without undoing everything.
💐 Bouquet Card  Hard · about 22 minutes
Open it and a whole bunch of flowers fans out. The best get-well card there is.
You need: 1 sheet of card · Coloured paper · Green paper · Scissors · Glue stick
  1. Fold the card in half.
  2. Cut five strips of green paper about 8 cm long for the stems.
  3. Glue all five stems to the inside of the card so they fan out from one point near the bottom.
  4. Cut five small flowers, each from a folded square so the petals match.
  5. Glue one flower to the top of each stem — but glue ONLY the centre, so the petals stay loose.
  6. Cut a paper band and glue it across where the stems meet, like a ribbon holding the bunch.
  7. Fold the petals up towards the middle so the card can shut.
  8. Write your message beside the bouquet.
Tip: Glue only the middle of each flower. Glue the petals down and the card will not close.

🎉 Party Decorations

Make the room look ready

⛓️ Paper Chain  Easy · about 10 minutes
The one everybody knows. Still the fastest way to make a room look like a party.
You need: Coloured paper · Scissors · Glue stick or sticky tape
  1. Cut the paper into strips about 3 cm wide and 15 cm long. Cut lots.
  2. Take one strip, bend it into a loop and glue the ends together. Hold for ten seconds.
  3. Thread the next strip through that loop before you glue its ends.
  4. Keep going, always threading through the last loop first.
  5. Change colour every few links so it makes a pattern.
  6. When it is as long as you want, tape each end up high.
Tip: Cut every strip before you start glueing. Stopping to cut breaks the rhythm.
🪭 Concertina Fan  Easy · about 6 minutes
Fold, staple, open. Useful on a hot day and it takes about five minutes.
You need: 1 sheet of paper · A stapler or sticky tape · Crayons
  1. Decorate both sides of the paper first — once it is folded you cannot reach the inside.
  2. Lay it landscape and fold 2 cm over from the short edge.
  3. Turn the paper over and fold another 2 cm the other way.
  4. Keep turning and folding until the whole sheet is a concertina strip.
  5. Squeeze one end tightly and staple or tape it so it cannot open.
  6. Fan out the other end.
Tip: Keep every fold the same width by folding the last one over as a measure.
🎏 Bunting Flags  Easy · about 15 minutes
A line of triangles across the room. Write a letter on each one to spell a name.
You need: Coloured paper · Scissors · String · Glue stick · Pens
  1. Cut a triangle from card to use as a template — about 15 cm across the top.
  2. Draw round it on coloured paper and cut out as many flags as you need.
  3. Write one big letter on each flag if you are spelling something.
  4. Lay the string out flat on the table.
  5. Fold the top 2 cm of each flag over the string.
  6. Glue that folded flap down onto the back of the flag, trapping the string inside.
  7. Space the flags evenly and let them dry before you hang it.
Tip: Fold the flap over the string rather than glueing the flag flat to it — it hangs much better.
🏵️ Paper Rosette  Medium · about 12 minutes
A big pleated medal for the wall, or a smaller one to pin on a birthday jumper.
You need: 2 sheets of paper · Scissors · Glue · A circle of card for the middle
  1. Fold each sheet into a concertina with folds about 1.5 cm wide.
  2. Fold each folded strip in half and glue the two inside edges together, so each sheet becomes a half circle.
  3. Glue the two half circles to each other to make one full circle.
  4. Press it flat and hold until the glue grabs.
  5. Glue the card circle over the middle to hide the joins.
  6. Cut two ribbon shapes and glue them behind the bottom edge.
Tip: Every pleat must be the same width or the circle will not close neatly.
🏮 Paper Lantern  Easy · about 8 minutes
One sheet, a row of cuts, and it becomes a round lantern. Never put a real candle in it.
You need: 1 sheet of coloured paper · Scissors · Glue or tape · String
  1. Cut a strip 3 cm wide off one long side and put it aside — that becomes the handle.
  2. Fold the rest of the sheet in half the long way.
  3. Cut from the FOLDED edge towards the open edges, stopping 3 cm short. Make a cut every 2 cm.
  4. Open the sheet out flat.
  5. Bend it round into a tube so the two short edges meet, and glue them together.
  6. Squash it gently from the top and bottom so the middle bulges out.
  7. Glue the handle strip across the top.

🎊 Pom-Pom Garland  Medium · about 18 minutes
Fluffy paper balls in a row. It looks far harder than it is.
You need: Tissue paper · Scissors · String · Thread or thin wire
  1. Cut the tissue into rectangles about 15 cm by 20 cm. You need six per pom-pom.
  2. Stack six and fold the stack into a concertina with 2 cm folds.
  3. Tie thread tightly round the middle of the folded strip, leaving long ends.
  4. Round both ends of the strip with scissors.
  5. Open it out and separate the layers one at a time, fluffing as you go.
  6. Make five or six pom-poms.
  7. Tie each one onto the long string by its thread ends, spaced a hand apart.

⭐ Star Garland  Medium · about 16 minutes
Puffy 3D stars strung in a line. They catch the light from both sides.
You need: Card or thick paper · Scissors · A pencil · Glue · String · A needle (ask a grown-up)
  1. Cut out two identical stars for every finished star you want.
  2. On each star, draw a line from every point to the middle.
  3. Fold along each of those lines so the star is not flat any more — points up, valleys between.

  5. Ask a grown-up to thread a needle and pass the string through the top point of each star.
  6. Slide the stars along the string and space them out.
  7. Tie a loop at each end for hanging.

❄️ Six-Point Snowflake  Medium · about 10 minutes
Real snowflakes have six sides, not four or eight. This is how you fold a proper one.
You need: 1 square of thin white paper · Scissors · A pencil
  1. Fold the square corner to corner into a triangle.
  2. Fold that triangle in half again into a smaller triangle. Find the middle of the long edge and pinch it.
  3. Fold the left third across to the right, using the pinch as the pivot.
  4. Fold the right third across on top of it. You now have a narrow wedge with all six sides stacked.
  5. Cut straight across the bottom to tidy the ragged ends.
  6. Snip shapes out of both long sides of the wedge — triangles, squares, curves. Do not cut all the way across.
  7. Open it out very carefully and press it flat under a book.
Tip: Cut more away than feels safe. Timid cuts make a snowflake that just looks like a circle.
🍽️ Table Place Cards  Easy · about 10 minutes
Little folded tents with everybody's name on. They make an ordinary dinner feel like an occasion.

  4. Draw something small next to each name that suits that person.
  5. Cut a tiny shape from coloured paper and glue it on as decoration.
  6. Stand one on each plate before everybody sits down.
Tip: Write the name on the FRONT of the tent while it is folded, so it ends up the right way up.
🌼 Giant Wall Flower  Hard · about 30 minutes
A flower as big as your head for the wall behind the birthday table.

  1. Cut eight big petal shapes from the paper — each one a long teardrop about 25 cm.

⚙️ Toys That Move

Spin, flap, pop and jump

🌀 Pinwheel  Medium · about 12 minutes
Hold it out of a car window or just blow. Either way it spins.
You need: 1 square of paper · A pencil with a rubber on the end · A push pin (a grown-up job) · A small paper bead or button · Scissors
  1. Draw two lines corner to corner across the square so you have an X.
  2. Cut along each line from the corner towards the middle, but STOP about 2 cm short of the centre.
  3. You now have four triangles each with two points at the corner.
  4. Take the LEFT point of the first triangle and bend it into the centre. Do not crease it — leave it curved.
  5. Do the same with the left point of the other three, so all four meet in the middle.
  6. Hold all four points down with your thumb.
  7. Ask a grown-up to push the pin through all four points and the centre, then into the pencil rubber.
  8. Slide the little bead onto the pin first if you have one, so the wheel spins freely.
Tip: Do not crease the folded points. A curved petal catches the wind; a creased one does not.
🔄 Spinning Top  Medium · about 8 minutes
A disc and a toothpick. Time whose top spins longest.
You need: Thick card · A toothpick or short pencil · Scissors · Crayons · A coin to draw round
  1. Draw round the coin on the card twice and cut out both circles.
  2. Colour the top of one circle in sections, like a wheel — the colours blur when it spins.
  3. Make a small hole in the exact centre of both circles.
  4. Push the toothpick through both, so they sit together as one thicker disc.
  5. Slide the disc down until it is a third of the way up the toothpick.
  6. Spin it on a smooth flat surface.
Tip: The hole must be dead centre or it will wobble and stop. Fold the circle in half twice to find the middle.
🐦 Flapping Bird  Hard · about 22 minutes
Pull its tail and the wings really flap. The crane's clever cousin.
You need: 1 square of thin paper (15 cm)
  1. Make the square base exactly as for the crane: fold both ways, turn over, fold corner to corner both ways, then collapse it into a small square.
  2. With the loose points at the bottom, fold the lower edges of the top layer in to the centre, fold the top triangle down, then open all three out again.
  3. Lift the bottom point up and press the sides in to make a long diamond. Repeat on the back.
  4. Fold the lower edges in to the centre line on both sides.
  5. Pull the two inner points out sideways and crease them so they stay put.
  6. Bend the tip of one down to make a beak — that one is the head.
  7. Now the difference from the crane: fold the wings DOWN flat against the body instead of opening them out.
  8. Hold the body under the wings with one hand and pull the tail gently with the other. The wings flap.
Tip: Hold the body, not the wings, or nothing will move.
🚁 Paper Helicopter  Easy · about 6 minutes
Drop it from the top of the stairs and it spins all the way down.
You need: 1 strip of paper about 5 cm by 20 cm · Scissors · 1 paper clip
  1. Cut a slit down the middle from the TOP, stopping halfway.
  2. Cut a short slit in from each side, about a third of the way up from the bottom.
  3. Fold the two bottom side flaps inwards over each other so the base is narrow.
  4. Fold the very bottom up and slide the paper clip on to hold it.
  5. Fold one top blade towards you and the other one away from you.
  6. Hold it up as high as you safely can and drop it.
Tip: Both blades must fold in OPPOSITE directions. Fold them the same way and it just falls.
🕺 Jumping Jack Puppet  Hard · about 25 minutes
Pull the string and its arms and legs fly up. A toy from two hundred years ago that still works.
You need: Thick card · Scissors · 4 paper fasteners (or knotted string) · String · A hole punch or a pencil · Pens
  1. Cut five pieces from card: one body, two arms and two legs. Make the arms and legs a bit longer than you think.
  2. Punch a hole at the top of each arm and leg, and a matching hole on the body at each shoulder and hip.
  3. Punch a SECOND hole in each arm and leg, just to the inside of the first one.
  4. Join each limb to the body through the first holes with a paper fastener. Do not do them up tightly — the limbs must swing.
  5. Turn it over. Tie a short string across between the two arms, threading through their second holes.
  6. Tie another short string across between the two legs the same way.
  7. Tie one long string to the middle of the arm string, then to the middle of the leg string, and let the rest hang below.
  8. Decorate the front, hang it up by the head, and pull the long string.
Tip: The two cross strings must be slack when the limbs hang down, or nothing will jump.
🥤 Cup and Ball Game  Medium · about 12 minutes
Swing the ball, catch it in the cup. Much harder than it looks.
You need: A paper cup · String about 40 cm · Newspaper or scrap paper · Sticky tape · Sticky tape
  1. Scrunch the scrap paper into a tight ball about the size of a walnut.
  2. Wrap sticky tape all around it until it is firm and smooth.
  3. Tape one end of the string firmly to the ball.
  4. Make a small hole in the bottom of the cup with a pencil.
  5. Thread the other end of the string up through the hole from outside.
  6. Tie a big knot inside the cup so the string cannot pull back out, and tape over the knot.
  7. Decorate the outside of the cup.
  8. Hold the cup, swing the ball up, and try to catch it.
Tip: Shorter string is much easier. Start at 30 cm and lengthen it once you can catch it every time.
🖐️ Finger Puppets  Easy · about 10 minutes
A whole cast on one hand. Put on a show over the back of the sofa.
You need: Coloured paper · Scissors · Sticky tape · Pens · Scraps of wool or paper for hair
  1. Cut a strip of paper about 6 cm by 8 cm for each puppet.
  2. Wrap one round your finger to check the size, then tape it into a tube.
  3. Slide it off your finger.
  4. Draw a face on the front of the tube.
  5. Cut out ears, hair, a hat or a beak from scraps and tape them on.
  6. Make one for each finger so you have a whole family.
  7. Practise a different voice for each one.
Tip: Make the tube slightly loose. Too tight and it will not come off again.
🪗 Paper Spring  Easy · about 8 minutes
Two strips folded over each other make a bouncy paper spring. Stick one behind a card and it pops.
You need: 2 strips of paper the same width (about 2 cm) · Glue stick
  1. Glue the two strips together at one end so they make an L shape.
  2. Fold the bottom strip up over the top one, and press the crease.
  3. Now fold the other strip over that one, and press.
  4. Keep going, always folding the bottom strip over the top one.
  5. Do not pull it tight — the folds should stack up loosely.
  6. When you run out of paper, glue the last two ends together.
  7. Squash it down and let go.
Tip: Long strips make a long spring. Tape two strips end to end before you start for an extra bouncy one.
💥 Paper Popper  Medium · about 6 minutes
Swing it down hard and it makes a loud BANG. Best played outside.
You need: 1 sheet of paper (the bigger the louder)
  1. Fold the sheet in half the long way, crease it, and open it out.
  2. Fold all four corners in to that middle crease, so you have a long pointed shape.
  3. Fold the whole thing in half along the middle crease again.
  4. Fold it in half the other way to find the centre, then open that fold back out.
  5. Fold the two top corners down to the centre line so a triangle points down.
  6. Fold the whole thing in half backwards along the last crease.
  7. Hold the two loose bottom corners between finger and thumb and swing it down sharply through the air.
Tip: It only bangs if the inner flap can whip out. If it stays silent, loosen that pocket.
🔮 Fortune Teller  Medium · about 12 minutes
Pick a colour, pick a number, lift the flap. Everybody in the playground knows this one.
You need: 1 square of paper · Pens
  1. Fold the square corner to corner both ways and open it out, so you can see the middle.
  2. Fold all four corners in to the centre.
  3. Turn it over and fold all four corners in to the centre again.
  4. Fold the whole thing in half, open it, then fold it in half the other way.
  5. Slide your thumbs and first fingers under the four square flaps and push them together — it opens and closes like a beak.
  6. Open it flat again. Write a colour on each of the four outside squares.
  7. Write the numbers 1 to 8 on the triangles inside.
  8. Lift each triangle and write a silly fortune underneath.
  9. Fold it back up and go and find someone to try it on.
Tip: Write the fortunes last. Everything before that is folding, and folding over writing smudges it.

🚀 Flight &amp; Space

Things that fly, or pretend to

✈️ Dart Paper Plane  Easy · about 4 minutes
The fast one. Narrow nose, sharp wings, flies straight and hard.
You need: 1 sheet of A4 or Letter paper
  1. Fold the sheet in half the long way, crease it hard, and open it out.
  2. Fold the two top corners in to that middle crease so the top comes to a point.
  3. Fold the two new slanted edges in to the middle crease again. The nose gets very pointy.
  4. Fold the whole plane in half along the original middle crease, with the folded parts inside.
  5. Fold one wing down so its edge lines up with the bottom of the body, and crease.
  6. Turn it over and fold the other wing exactly the same.
  7. Open the wings out flat and throw it hard and level.
Tip: If it dives, bend the very back edge of both wings up by a few millimetres.
🛩️ Glider Plane  Medium · about 8 minutes
The slow one. Wide wings, blunt nose, floats right across the room.
You need: 1 sheet of paper · A paper clip
  1. Fold the sheet in half the long way and open it out.
  2. Fold the two top corners in to the middle crease.
  3. Fold the whole pointed top down so the point touches the middle of the paper. It now has a blunt nose.
  4. Fold the two new top corners in to the middle again, leaving a small triangle sticking out below.
  5. Fold that little triangle up over them — it locks the folds shut.
  6. Fold the whole plane in half backwards along the middle crease.
  7. Fold each wing down, but make them WIDE — fold about 2 cm from the body, not at the edge.
  8. Slide the paper clip onto the nose and throw it gently, level, with a slow push.
Tip: Throw a glider gently. Thrown hard it just loops and crashes; this one wants a push, not a fling.
🚀 Paper Rocket  Medium · about 12 minutes
A rolled tube with a nose cone and fins. Blow through a straw and it flies.
You need: Paper · Sticky tape · Scissors · A pencil to roll round · A drinking straw
  1. Roll a piece of paper tightly around the pencil and tape the long edge so it stays a tube.
  2. Slide it off the pencil.
  3. Cut a circle of paper, cut one slit from the edge to the middle, then overlap the two sides into a cone and tape it.
  4. Tape the cone onto one end of the tube. Make sure no air can get out — that end must be sealed.
  5. Cut three fin shapes and tape them evenly around the other end.
  6. Decorate the rocket.
  7. Slide the open end over the straw, blow hard, and launch it. Point it away from people.
Tip: The nose cone must be airtight. If it leaks, the rocket will barely move.
⭕ Ring Glider  Medium · about 10 minutes
It looks like it cannot possibly fly, and then it sails right across the garden.
You need: Paper · A drinking straw or a thin stick · Sticky tape · Scissors
  1. Cut two strips of paper: one 3 cm wide and one 5 cm wide.
  2. Curl the narrow strip into a small ring and tape the ends together with a 2 cm overlap.
  3. Curl the wide strip into a bigger ring and tape it the same way.
  4. Lay the straw down. Tape the small ring near one end, standing up like a wheel.
  5. Tape the big ring at the other end the same way.
  6. Check both rings are square to the straw and facing the same way.
  7. Hold the straw in the middle, small ring in front, and throw it like a dart.
Tip: Small ring at the FRONT. Throw it the other way round and it will just tumble.
🪂 Parachute Jumper  Easy · about 10 minutes
Drop it from the landing and watch it float all the way down.
You need: A square of thin paper or a plastic bag · 4 pieces of string the same length · Sticky tape · A small toy or a paper figure
  1. Cut the square as big as you can — bigger falls slower.
  2. Tape one piece of string to each of the four corners.
  3. Gather the four loose ends together and check they are all the same length.
  4. Tie all four ends together in one knot.
  5. Tape or tie the knot to your toy jumper.
  6. Fold the parachute loosely, hold it by the middle, and drop it from as high as you can safely reach.
Tip: If it drops like a stone, snip a small hole in the very centre. It sounds wrong, but it makes it steadier.
🪃 Paper Boomerang  Hard · about 15 minutes
Throw it indoors and it comes back to you. It takes practice, and it is worth it.
You need: Thick card (a cereal box is ideal) · Scissors · A ruler · A pencil
  1. Draw three identical arms on the card, each about 10 cm long and 2 cm wide, with rounded ends.
  2. Cut them out carefully — straight edges matter here.
  3. Lay one arm flat and glue the second across it at an angle, crossing at the centre.
  4. Glue the third across both, so all three arms are evenly spaced like a fan. Let it dry.
  5. Bend the tip of each arm up very slightly — just a few millimetres of twist, all in the same direction.
  6. Hold it upright between finger and thumb, tilted slightly back.
  7. Flick it forwards with your wrist, indoors, in a big room. It should curve round and come back.
Tip: All three tips must twist the SAME way. This is the whole secret, and the reason a wrong one never returns.
🪐 Solar System Mobile  Hard · about 30 minutes
All eight planets hanging in order, with the Sun in the middle. A real bedroom-ceiling project.
You need: Card · Compass or round things to draw round · Scissors · Crayons · Thread · A garden stick, or a wire coat hanger — ask a grown-up to bend it and check the ends are not sharp
  1. Draw and cut circles for the Sun and all eight planets. Keep the sizes in order: Jupiter biggest, Mercury smallest.
  2. Colour both sides of each one — they will spin, so the back shows too.
  3. Cut a thin ring of card for Saturn and glue it around the middle of that planet.
  4. Write each planet's name on the back in pencil so you remember which is which.
  5. Make a small hole in the top of each planet and thread a piece of string through.
  6. Tie the Sun in the centre of the stick, hanging lowest.
  7. Tie the planets along the stick in order out from the Sun: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune.
  8. Balance it by sliding the strings along the stick, then hang it up.
Tip: Order matters more than size here. Get the order right and it teaches something every time you look up.
👨‍🚀 Astronaut Helmet  Medium · about 18 minutes
A helmet big enough to actually wear, from one paper bag or a sheet of card.
You need: A large paper bag or a big sheet of card · Scissors · Sticky tape · Tin foil (optional) · Pens
  1. If you are using card, roll it into a tube that fits loosely over your head and tape it.

🌟 Shooting Star Wand  Easy · about 8 minutes
A star on a stick with a tail of streamers. Wave it and the tail follows.

  1. Cut two identical stars from card.

🪁 Paper Kite  Hard · about 25 minutes
A real kite that really flies, from paper and two thin sticks.
You need: A big sheet of paper · 2 thin sticks (bamboo skewers work; ask a grown-up to blunt the points) · String · Sticky tape · Scissors · Ribbon for the tail
  1. Lay the two sticks in a cross, the short one about a third of the way down the long one.

Hats, masks, crowns and cuffs

Every birthday needs one. Sized to fit whoever is wearing it.

  1. Wrap the strip round the head of whoever will wear it and mark where it overlaps. Do not tape it yet.

  1. Fold the card in half and draw half a pair of glasses against the fold, including the bridge over the nose.

📦 Origami Box with Lid  Hard · about 20 minutes
A real box with a lid that fits, from two squares and no glue at all.
You need: 2 squares of paper — the lid square must be 5 mm bigger each way than the base square
  1. Take the base square. Fold it corner to corner both ways and open it out.
  2. Fold all four corners in to the centre.
  3. Fold the top edge down to the centre, then the bottom edge up to the centre. Open both out again.
  4. Do the same with the left and right edges, then open them out. You should see a grid of creases.
  5. Unfold the left and right corners only.
  6. Fold the left and right edges in to the centre, then lift them up so two walls stand.
  7. Fold the two pointed ends over the tops of the walls and tuck them down inside. The base is done.
  8. Make the lid exactly the same way with the bigger square, and slide it on top.
Tip: The lid square MUST be slightly bigger or the lid will not go on. Cut 5 mm off two sides of the base square if you only have same-size paper.
✉️ One-Sheet Envelope  Easy · about 6 minutes
An envelope from one square, in four folds. Learn it once and you never need to buy one.
You need: 1 square of paper · Glue stick or a sticker
  1. Put the square down as a diamond, pattern facing down.
  2. Fold the bottom corner up to just past the middle.
  3. Fold the left corner in across it.
  4. Fold the right corner in across that, so the two overlap.
  5. Put a little glue on the overlap and press it.
  6. Slide your letter in from the top.
  7. Fold the top corner down and seal it with a sticker.
Tip: Fold the bottom corner PAST the middle, not exactly to it, or the envelope will be too shallow to hold anything.

🧺 Woven Paper Basket  Medium · about 20 minutes
Weave strips over and under and a flat sheet becomes a basket that holds things.
You need: Coloured paper in two colours · Scissors · Glue stick · Ruler
  1. Fold one sheet in half and cut slits from the fold towards the open edge, stopping 3 cm short. Make them 2 cm apart.
  2. Open it out. You have a sheet with a row of slots down the middle.
  3. Cut the second colour into strips 2 cm wide.
  4. Weave the first strip through the slots: over, under, over, under.
  5. Weave the next strip the opposite way: under, over, under, over. That alternating is what makes it hold together.
  6. Keep going until the sheet is full, then glue the ends of every strip down at both sides.
  7. Bend the woven sheet up at all four sides and glue the corners together to make the basket walls.
  8. Cut one more strip and glue it across the top as a handle.
Tip: Every row must start opposite to the one before. Two rows the same and the weave falls apart.

A box shaped like a pyramid that closes with a ribbon. It holds a small present perfectly.

  1. Draw a square 12 cm across on the card.

🎲 Games to Play

Make it, then play it

🎲 Paper Dice  Medium · about 15 minutes
A real six-sided dice from a flat net. Handy when the dice from the board game goes missing.
You need: Card · Ruler · Pencil · Scissors · Glue · A black pen
  1. Draw a row of four squares, each 4 cm, side by side.
  2. Add one more square above the second one in the row, and one below it. You have a cross shape — that is a cube net.
  3. Add 1 cm glue flaps to the outside edges: one on each free edge that will need to join another.
  4. Cut the whole net out.
  5. Score along every fold line with a ruler and a dead pen.
  6. Draw the dots: 1 opposite 6, 2 opposite 5, 3 opposite 4. Every pair adds up to seven.
  7. Fold it into a cube and glue the flaps inside one at a time, leaving the last face until the end.
  8. Tuck the last flap in and hold it until the glue sets.
Tip: Opposite faces must add up to seven. That is true of every real dice, and it is what makes yours fair-looking.
🃏 Memory Match Cards  Easy · about 15 minutes
Make twenty cards, ten matching pairs, and play the oldest memory game there is.
You need: Card · Scissors · Ruler · Crayons
  1. Cut twenty rectangles of card the same size — about 6 cm by 8 cm. Cut them together in a stack so they match.
  2. Think of ten simple pictures: a star, a cat, a boat, a tree.
  3. Draw each picture on TWO cards, as alike as you can.
  4. Colour the backs of all twenty cards exactly the same, so you cannot tell them apart face down.
  5. Shuffle them and lay them all out face down in a grid.
  6. Turn two over. If they match, keep them and go again. If not, turn them back and let the next player go.
  7. The winner has the most pairs when all the cards are gone.
Tip: The backs must be identical. One smudge on one card and that card becomes findable.
🏈 Table Football Flicker  Medium · about 8 minutes
Fold a paper triangle and flick it across the table between two goalposts made of fingers.
You need: 1 sheet of paper · Scissors
  1. Cut a strip of paper about 5 cm wide and as long as the sheet.
  2. Fold the strip in half lengthways so it is narrow and strong.
  3. Fold one corner across to make a triangle at the end.
  4. Keep folding that triangle over and over along the strip, like folding a flag.
  5. When you get near the end, tuck the last little bit into the pocket of the triangle.
  6. To play, stand it up on its point at the table edge and flick it with a finger.
  7. The other player makes a goal with two thumbs and their first fingers.
Tip: Tucking the last bit in is what stops it unravelling. Trim it first if it is too fat to tuck.
🎳 Paper Skittles  Medium · about 18 minutes
Ten paper cones in a triangle and a scrunched paper ball. A bowling alley in the hallway.
You need: 10 sheets of paper · Sticky tape · Scrap paper for the ball · Crayons · Small stones or coins to weigh the cones
  1. Roll one sheet into a cone and tape the edge. Trim the bottom flat so it stands up.
  2. Make ten of them.
  3. Number the cones 1 to 10 and colour them.
  4. Drop a stone or a coin into each cone so a small breeze does not knock them over.
  5. Scrunch scrap paper into a firm ball and tape it smooth.
  6. Set the cones out in a triangle: one at the front, then two, then three, then four.

🐍 Snakes &amp; Ladders Board  Medium · about 25 minutes
Draw the board, make the counters, and you have a game that lasts for years.
You need: A big sheet of paper or card · Ruler · Pencil · Crayons · Card scraps for counters · A dice (make one!)
  1. Draw a grid ten squares across and ten squares down.
  2. Number the squares from 1 in the bottom left, going right along the bottom row.
  3. Number the next row from right to left, and keep zigzagging up to 100 in the top left.
  4. Draw five ladders joining a lower square to a higher one.

  7. Cut small circles of card for counters and colour one for each player.

🁣 Paper Dominoes  Easy · about 15 minutes
A full set of twenty-eight dominoes. Play the game, or stand them up and topple them.
You need: Thick card · Ruler · Scissors · A black pen
  1. Cut twenty-eight rectangles of card, each about 4 cm by 8 cm.
  2. Draw a line across the middle of each one.
  3. Now the dots. Start with double blank, then blank-1, blank-2, up to blank-6.
  4. Then do 1-1, 1-2, up to 1-6. Then start at 2-2, then 3-3, and so on.
  5. That gives every pair exactly once: twenty-eight in all.

🎯 Ring Toss  Medium · about 15 minutes
Rolled paper rings and a stand to throw them onto. Score more for the harder pegs.
You need: Paper · Sticky tape · A cardboard box or thick card base · Scissors · Crayons
  1. Roll a sheet of paper diagonally into a long thin tube and tape it.
  2. Bend the tube into a circle, push one end inside the other, and tape it. That is one ring.
  3. Make five rings and colour them.
  4. For the pegs, roll three shorter tubes and tape each one.
  5. Cut three slits in the box lid and push a peg into each, taping underneath so they stand upright.
  6. Write a score on the base next to each peg: 1 for the near one, 5 for the far one.

🧩 Design a Maze  Medium · about 20 minutes
Draw a maze so tricky that a grown-up gets stuck in it. Swap with a friend and race.
You need: Squared paper if you have it, or plain paper and a ruler · Pencil · Rubber · A black pen
  1. Mark a START on one edge and a FINISH on the other.
  2. In pencil, draw ONE path that wanders from start to finish. Make it turn a lot.
  3. Now draw walls on both sides of that path, all the way along.
  4. Fill the empty space with dead ends: paths that branch off and stop.
  5. Make the dead ends long. Short ones are too easy to spot.
  6. Go over all the walls in black pen.
  7. Rub out your original pencil path so the answer is hidden.
  8. Test it yourself before you hand it over.
Tip: Draw the correct path FIRST and the dead ends after. Mazes drawn the other way round usually turn out to have no solution at all.
🏗️ Card Tower Challenge  Hard · about 20 minutes
Make a deck of blank cards and see how high a tower you can build before it falls.
You need: Thick card · Scissors · Ruler · A steady table · A ruler to measure your record
  1. Cut about forty rectangles of card the same size, roughly 6 cm by 9 cm.
  2. Make sure they are all the same. A tower is only as steady as its wobbliest card.
  3. Lean two cards against each other in an upside-down V. That is one arch.
  4. Build a row of arches side by side, all touching.
  5. Lay cards flat across the top of the whole row to make a floor.
  6. Build the next row of arches on top of that floor, slightly fewer than the row below.
  7. Keep going until it falls, then measure how high you got.
  8. For a harder version, cut small slits in the card edges so they slot together instead.
Tip: Breathe out slowly and let go with both hands at once. Most towers fall because of the person, not the cards.

  3. Number the strips backwards, so the last day is number 1.
  4. Turn the first strip so the writing faces IN, and glue it into a loop.
  5. Thread the next strip through and glue it, writing facing in again.
  6. Keep going, ending with number 1 at the bottom.
  7. Hang it up and tear off the bottom link each day.
Tip: Writing faces inwards so nobody can read ahead. That is the whole point of the chain.

💌 Cards for Occasions

Handmade cards for holidays and big days

🎈 Birthday Balloon Card  Easy · about 12 minutes
Open it and a bunch of balloons springs up off the page.
You need: 1 sheet of card · Coloured paper · Scissors · Glue stick · Pens · Thin string or thread
  1. Fold the card in half and put it down with the fold at the top.
  2. Cut four balloon shapes from different coloured papers, each about as big as your thumb.
  3. Cut four short pieces of paper about 1 cm wide and 5 cm long for the springs.
  4. Fold each spring strip back and forth like a tiny concertina.
  5. Glue one end of each spring to the inside of the card, spread out near the top.
  6. Glue a balloon to the free end of each spring, so the balloons stand up off the page.
  7. Draw a curly string from each balloon down to one point near the bottom.
  8. Write your message underneath, then close the card gently to check the balloons squash flat.
Tip: Squash the springs flat with your hand before you close it the first time. If a balloon pokes out of the side, trim it smaller.
🍎 Thank You, Teacher  Easy · about 10 minutes
An apple-shaped card for the last day of term.
You need: Red card · Green paper · Brown paper · Scissors · Glue stick · A pen
  1. Fold the red card in half.
  2. Draw half an apple shape against the folded edge, so the fold runs down the middle of the apple.
  3. Cut it out, but do NOT cut along the fold, or the card will fall into two pieces.
  4. Open it up. You have an apple that opens like a book.
  5. Cut a small leaf from green paper and a short stalk from brown paper.
  6. Glue the stalk and the leaf to the top of the front.
  7. Write your thank you inside, and say one thing you actually enjoyed this year.
Tip: Naming one real thing beats 'thanks for everything'. Teachers keep the ones that mention something they did.
🩹 Get Well Soon Card  Easy · about 10 minutes
A cheerful card with a giant paper plaster across the front.
You need: Card · Pale pink or cream paper · Coloured paper · Scissors · Glue stick · Pens
  1. Fold the card in half.
  2. Cut a long rectangle from the pale paper and round off its four corners. That is the plaster.
  3. Cut a small rectangle of a different colour and glue it across the middle of the plaster.
  4. Draw a row of little dots on that middle patch, like a real plaster.
  5. Glue the plaster diagonally across the front of the card.
  6. Draw a smiley face in one corner.
  7. Inside, write something that will make them laugh rather than something serious.
Tip: A joke from the Kids and Family Jokes category makes a better get-well card than a long message.
⭐ Congratulations Star Card  Easy · about 10 minutes
A gold star that stands proud of the card. Good for a race won or a badge earned.
You need: Card · Gold or yellow paper · Scissors · Glue stick · Pens
  1. Fold the card in half.
  2. Cut two identical stars from the gold paper.
  3. Fold one star gently down its middle so it is not flat.
  4. Glue only the LEFT half of the folded star onto the front of the card.
  5. Glue the second star flat, just behind and a little to one side, so it peeps out.
  6. The folded star now lifts off the card when you look from the side.
  7. Write what they did on the front, and why you are proud of them inside.
Tip: Glue only one half of the folded star. Glue both and it lies flat and loses the whole effect.
💐 Mother's Day Flower Pot  Medium · about 18 minutes
A pot on the front with real paper flowers you can lift out. For a mum, a nan, or whoever does the mum jobs.
You need: Card · Coloured paper · Green paper · Scissors · Glue stick · Pens
  1. Fold the card in half.
  2. Cut a pot shape from coloured paper, about as wide as your hand.
  3. Glue the pot to the front, but put glue ONLY around the sides and the bottom. Leave the top open so it is a pocket.
  4. Cut three strips of green paper for stems, each a little longer than the pot is tall.
  5. Cut three flowers, each from a folded square so the petals match all the way round.
  6. Glue one flower to the top of each stem, and a small circle in the middle of each flower.
  7. Write one thing you love about them on each stem.
  8. Slide the stems into the pot pocket so the flowers can be pulled out and read.
Tip: Making the pot a pocket is the whole idea. Glue the top edge by mistake and the flowers are stuck.
👔 Father's Day Shirt Card  Medium · about 16 minutes
A card folded into a shirt with a collar and a tie. For a dad, a grandad, or whoever does the dad jobs.
You need: 1 sheet of card · Coloured paper for the tie · Scissors · Glue stick · Pens · A ruler
  1. Lay the card portrait and fold the bottom edge up to the middle.
  2. Turn it over. Fold the two top corners down towards the middle so they nearly meet, leaving a small gap.
  3. Those two folded triangles are the collar.
  4. Fold the top edge of the bottom half up and tuck it UNDER the collar.
  5. Cut a tie shape from the coloured paper: a small knot and a longer pointed piece.
  6. Glue the knot into the gap between the collar points, then glue the tie hanging below it.
  7. Draw a line of buttons down the middle and a pocket on one side.
  8. Write your message on the back, or inside if you left it openable.
Tip: Press every fold hard with your thumbnail. This one only looks like a shirt if the folds are sharp.
🎄 Christmas Tree Pop-Up  Medium · about 16 minutes
A tree that stands straight up when the card opens, with baubles you stick on yourself.
You need: 1 sheet of card · Green paper · Coloured paper scraps · Scissors · Glue stick · Ruler
  1. Fold the card in half and open it flat again so you can see the middle crease.
  2. Cut two parallel slits into the crease, 5 cm apart and 4 cm deep.
  3. Fold the flap between the slits forwards, then close the card and press it so the flap pops inwards.
  4. Open the card. A step now stands up off the middle.
  5. Cut a triangle of green paper a little taller than the step and glue its bottom edge to the front of the step.
  6. Cut a second, smaller green triangle and glue it over the first, slightly higher up.
  7. Stick small circles of coloured paper on as baubles, and a star on the top.
  8. Close the card and check the tree folds flat inside. Trim the tree if it pokes out.
Tip: Measure the two slits with a ruler. This is one of the few paper jobs where guessing really does show.
🪔 Diwali Diya Card  Medium · about 16 minutes
A row of little clay lamps with glowing flames, for the festival of lights.
You need: Dark blue or purple card · Orange and yellow paper · Brown paper · Scissors · Glue stick · A gold or white pen
  1. Fold the dark card in half.
  2. Cut three small bowl shapes from the brown paper. These are the diyas, the little lamps.
  3. Glue them in a row across the front, near the bottom.
  4. Cut three flame shapes from orange paper and three smaller ones from yellow.
  5. Glue an orange flame above each lamp, then a yellow flame on top of each orange one.
  6. Use the gold or white pen to draw a ring of dots glowing around each flame.
  7. Draw a rangoli pattern along the bottom edge: a row of repeating shapes, mirrored on both sides.
  8. Write Happy Diwali inside.
Tip: A rangoli pattern is symmetrical. Fold your paper in half, draw half the pattern, and copy it to the other side.
🌙 Eid Crescent Moon Card  Medium · about 15 minutes
A silver moon and a sky of stars, for Eid.
You need: Dark blue card · White or silver paper · Yellow paper · Scissors · Glue stick · A white pen
  1. Fold the dark blue card in half.
  2. To make the crescent, draw a circle on the white paper, then draw a second circle overlapping it and slightly to one side.
  3. Cut around the outside of the first circle, then cut away the part where the second circle overlaps.
  4. What is left is a crescent moon. Glue it near the top of the front.
  5. Cut five or six small stars from the yellow paper and glue them around the moon.
  6. Use the white pen to add tiny dots between the stars so the sky looks full.
  7. Write Eid Mubarak on the front, and your message inside.
Tip: The two circles must overlap, not just touch. The further you slide the second circle across, the thinner the crescent.
🕸️ Halloween Web Card  Medium · about 15 minutes
A card with a real woven web across the front and a friendly spider sitting on it.
You need: Black card · White wool or string · Scissors · Sticky tape · White and coloured paper · A hole punch
  1. Fold the black card in half.
  2. On the front, punch a ring of holes around the edge, about 2 cm apart.
  3. Cut a long piece of white wool and tape one end inside the front cover.
  4. Thread the wool out through one hole, across the front, and back in through a hole on the other side.
  5. Keep crossing from side to side so the threads make a web pattern.
  6. Tape the loose end inside when the web looks full.
  7. Cut a small black circle and glue two white paper eyes on it. That is the spider.
  8. Glue the spider onto the web, and add eight short legs cut from paper.
Tip: Cross the middle a few times early on. Threads that only go round the outside look like a fence, not a web.
💗 Woven Heart Card  Hard · about 22 minutes
Two paper hearts woven together so they lock without any glue at all. A proper Valentine.
You need: Red paper · White paper · Scissors · A ruler · A pencil · Card for the base
  1. Cut one rectangle of red paper and one of white, both the same size, about 8 cm by 16 cm.
  2. Fold each one in half so you have two squares standing on their folds.
  3. Round off the two corners at the OPEN end of each, so each piece looks like an arch.
  4. On each piece, cut two straight slits from the folded edge up towards the curve, dividing it into three equal strips. Stop before you reach the curve.
  5. Hold the red arch in one hand and the white in the other, at right angles to each other.
  6. Weave the first red strip THROUGH the first white strip, then AROUND the second, then through the third.
  7. Weave the second red strip the opposite way: around, through, around.
  8. Weave the third strip like the first. Open it out and you have a heart-shaped pocket that holds itself together.
  9. Glue it to the front of a folded card, and slide a small note inside the pocket.
Tip: Alternating is the whole trick: through, around, through, then around, through, around. Two rows the same and it falls apart.
🕎 Hanukkah Menorah Card  Hard · about 20 minutes
A menorah with nine candles, and a flame you add each night of the festival.
You need: Blue card · White and yellow paper · Scissors · Glue stick · A silver or white pen · A small envelope or paper pocket
  1. Fold the blue card in half.
  2. Cut a menorah shape from white paper: a base, an upright stem, and a straight arm across the top.
  3. Glue it to the front of the card, low down, so there is room above it.
  4. Cut nine thin candles from white paper, all the same height except the middle one, which should be taller.
  5. Glue the candles along the arm, four on each side and the tall one in the middle.
  6. Cut nine small flames from yellow paper, but do NOT glue them on.
  7. Glue a small paper pocket inside the card and keep the flames in it.
  8. Each night of Hanukkah, take out one flame and stick it on. The tall middle candle, the shamash, is lit first every night.
Tip: Leaving the flames loose is what makes this one worth keeping. It becomes something the family does together for eight nights.
