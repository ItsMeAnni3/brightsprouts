STEM Activities for Kids

Twenty things to do together that turn a kitchen table into a lab. Every one teaches a real idea in Science, Technology, Engineering or Math, and every one uses things a family already has or can easily find.

Four ways in

🧪 STEM Activities
Twenty hands-on activities, five each in Kitchen Chemistry, Forces & Motion, Build & Engineer, and Math & Patterns. Every one shows a picture of what you are aiming at, the materials, and the steps in order.
🔬 Think Like a Scientist
The same five-step pattern real scientists and engineers use every day: ask a question, guess an answer, test it, observe what happens, and draw a conclusion. Science is a loop, not a straight line.

Six of the twenty activities

🌋 Baking Soda Volcano (Kitchen Chemistry, Easy, 15 min)
Chemical reactions: mixing two things can make a brand new gas.
🌈 Rainbow Density Tower (Kitchen Chemistry, Medium, 20 min)
Density: some liquids are heavier for their size than others.
🥚 Egg Drop Challenge (Forces & Motion, Medium, 30 min)
Force and impact: padding changes how hard a landing hits.
🍡 Spaghetti & Marshmallow Tower (Build & Engineer, Medium, 25 min)
Structural engineering: why triangles hold shapes up better than squares.
🧭 Make a Floating Compass (Build & Engineer, Easy, 15 min)
Magnetism: turning a needle into a magnet that finds north.
🌻 Fibonacci Nature Hunt (Math & Patterns, Easy, 20 min)
Math patterns hiding in real plants.

Safe for families

Every activity that needs a grown-up's hands, heat, an oven, a needle, choosing a drop height, launching something, says so plainly right where it happens, not in a footnote. Nothing on the list needs an open flame, a corrosive chemical, or a power tool.

Sample worksheet questions

1. What do the letters in STEM stand for?
Answer: Science, Technology, Engineering and Math
2. In the Egg Drop Challenge, what matters most: how far the egg falls, or how fast it stops?
Answer: How fast it stops
3. What shape makes the strongest tower in the Spaghetti Tower activity?
Answer: A triangle
4. Why does a balloon rocket zoom forward when air rushes out the back?
Answer: Newton's third law: every action has an equal and opposite reaction
5. If a recipe needs 1/4 cup and you double it, how much do you need?
Answer: 1/2 cup
